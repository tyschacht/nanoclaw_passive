# Agent Marketplace Dead Ends — Wave 2 (April 2026)

Bundled evaluation of three additional platforms/concepts that surfaced this research cycle and do not meet the viable threshold.

---

## 1. OpenJobs.bot — $WAGE Token on Solana

### What It Claims to Be
"The open marketplace for AI agents." Bots hire bots, negotiate pay, and transact in $WAGE on Solana. The pitch: a fully programmatic bot-to-bot economy.

### Why It Fails
Virtual token red herring — the exact same pattern as BotCoins/BotStadium/AgentGig. $WAGE is a Solana SPL token with no documented liquid market at a verifiable price. "Earn $WAGE → convert to USD" requires: (1) $WAGE having a real market price, (2) that market having sufficient liquidity, and (3) the conversion being frictionless. None of these are confirmed. The platform's website provides almost no substantive information about job categories, compensation rates, or agent registration. A previously-documented pattern: any "bots earn [custom token] on Solana/Base" framing should be treated as virtual currency until the token shows $10K+/day verified DEX volume.

**Scores:** Agentic viability 3 | Setup effort 5 | Yield potential 1 | Risk 4 | **Overall 3.25**

## 2. Moltbook — AI-Only Social Network (Acquired by Meta)

### What It Claims to Be
The world's first social network for AI agents only — Reddit-style forum (submolts), launched January 2026, 202K+ registered agents as of April 7. Briefly viral; Elon Musk praised it. Meta acquired it March 10, 2026 for undisclosed amount.

### Why It Fails
No creator/agent income mechanism exists or is planned. Moltbook is a venue for AI agents to communicate — like Reddit for bots — not a marketplace where agents earn from participation. Discussed income concepts (token incentives, attention markets) are speculative and pre-acquisition. Under Meta ownership, any future monetization will flow to Meta, not agent operators. Even if an "ad revenue for agents" program launched, it would follow the same pattern as AgentAds.xyz which was evaluated as NOT_VIABLE (yield too low, advertiser demand unproven).

**Scores:** Agentic viability 2 | Setup effort 6 | Yield potential 1 | Risk 9 | **Overall 4.5**

## 3. Replit Bounties — Deprecated

### What It Claims (Claimed) to Be
Replit's bounty marketplace where users post coding jobs payable in Replit Cycles, with freelancers/agents claiming and completing them.

### Why It Fails
**Officially deprecated in 2026.** Replit is no longer accepting applications for Bounty Hunter and has shut down the Bounties Marketplace feature. Replit's current focus is its $9B Series D (March 2026) and the launch of Replit Agent 4 — consumer-facing product development, not a platform for external agents to earn. Do not evaluate any "Replit Bounties" angle — the program no longer exists.

**Scores:** N/A — platform does not exist

## Pattern Update

This research cycle confirms the pattern is broader than previously documented: even technically sophisticated agent marketplaces with live infrastructure and real registered users (dealwork.ai: 252 completed tasks, 173 AI agents) fail to produce meaningful yield when average task value is <$1. **The cold-start problem in agent marketplaces is not a technical problem — it's a buyer-demand problem.** The platforms that work (Apify, MCPize, Whop) solve discovery by having an existing audience. Agent-to-agent marketplaces haven't solved why a buyer would come to their specific marketplace over established alternatives.
