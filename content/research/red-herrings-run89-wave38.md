# Wave 38 Bundle — Not Viable

Five ideas evaluated in the June 3, 2026 research run. All fail the agentic passive income test.

---

## 1. AgentChain (agtchain.io)

### What It Claims to Be
An "on-chain economy where AI agents transact autonomously." Agents post jobs, accept work, earn XAGT tokens, and build reputation scores. HN Show HN post (score 9, June 3, 2026). Dashboard where the human operator watches and withdraws.

### Why It Doesn't Work Agentically
- **XAGT token only** — No USD or USDC withdrawal path documented. Income is in a proprietary token with unproven value. 18th+ instance of token-only income red herring.
- **Cold-start marketplace** — HN comment response was "Why?" and comments were flagged. Zero documented transactions on the marketplace.
- **Circular economy problem**: Agents posting jobs AND earning from jobs in the same platform doesn't create external value; it's agents paying agents with tokens that have no external buyer.

### Scores
| Criterion | Score (1-10) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Agentic viability | 6 | Technical mechanism plausible |
| Setup effort | 5 | Requires on-chain wallet, XAGT acquisition |
| Yield potential | 1 | Token-only; no USD/USDC withdrawal documented |
| Risk | 5 | Smart contract/token risk |
| **Overall** | **4.25** | Token dependency is a structural disqualifier |

### Source
HN: 48369985 (Show HN, score 9, June 3 2026) — agtchain.io

## 2. Clawlancer (clawlancer.ai)

### What It Claims to Be
An "AI agent marketplace" using non-custodial USDC escrow on Base blockchain. Agents browse bounties, claim work, get paid automatically. Described in DEV.to "12 Platforms Where AI Agents Actually Earn Money" (May 2026) as having 268 agents and 52 tasks completed.

### Why It Doesn't Work Agentically
- **Cold-start volume**: 52 tasks completed across 268 agents = 0.19 tasks/agent total. Average task value cited as $5–$200. Best case $10 per agent over all time.
- **No verified income**: DEV.to article lists it alongside dealwork.ai and opentask.ai as "having pending bids" — not completed payouts. The USDC escrow mechanism appears functional but the marketplace has no buyer liquidity.
- **Structural cold-start**: Same pattern as BountyBook, ClawGig, TaskMarket, SwarmDock, 0xWork, etc. — good infrastructure, zero buyer demand. 19th+ instance of the pattern.

### Scores
| Criterion | Score (1-10) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Agentic viability | 7 | USDC escrow, headless API technically solid |
| Setup effort | 6 | API registration, USDC wallet |
| Yield potential | 2 | 52 tasks total, near-zero per-agent yield |
| Risk | 8 | Non-custodial, low ToS risk |
| **Overall** | **5.75** | Cold-start disqualifies |

### Source
DEV.to: "The Agent Economy Is Real: 12 Platforms Where AI Agents Actually Earn Money" (kirothebot, May 2026) — clawlancer.ai

## 3. AutoBox / Shepherd Stack — Agent-Run SEO Link Building

### What It Claims to Be
"The Autonomy Experiment" is a YouTube channel documenting a real longitudinal experiment where an AI agent called AutoBox runs a micro-SaaS business (writemyformula.com, $9/month for formula generation). Episode 5 documents the agent's first confirmed external business result: it conducted cold email outreach for SEO link trades and closed one real link exchange deal with a partner company. The agent identified relevant sites, sent cold emails, and negotiated a backlink deal entirely without human intervention.

### Why It Doesn't Work Agentically
- **Zero revenue documented**: The host explicitly states writemyformula.com has not made any money. A link trade is not revenue — it's a precursor to improved SEO, which might lead to traffic, which might lead to conversions, which might lead to revenue. Multiple conversion layers before income.
- **Indirect and very slow income horizon**: SEO link building → improved ranking → organic traffic → trial users → paid conversions. Best case $200–500/mo at 6–12 month timeline. Not passive income in any operational sense.
- **Agentic but pre-viable**: Cold email outreach is agentic (LLM writes personalized emails, tracks replies, follows up). The agent also needs human oversight for link placement decisions on the site itself. The *outreach* mechanism is real; the *business* isn't generating income.
- **Not a replicable income mechanism without a product**: AutoBox only works because there's an underlying micro-SaaS charging $9/month. If the product never gets traffic or conversions, link building is an elaborate exercise producing zero revenue.

### Scores
| Criterion | Score (1-10) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Agentic viability | 5 | Outreach agentic; product-building and link placement require human |
| Setup effort | 4 | Need existing micro-SaaS + domain authority + email domain warmup |
| Yield potential | 3 | $0 currently; $200-500/mo in 6-12 months best case |
| Risk | 8 | Low legal risk; standard cold email |
| **Overall** | **5.0** | No income generated; extremely slow horizon |

### Source
YouTube: The Shepherd Stack (f5o7NrCQ8EE, "The Autonomy Experiment Ep. 5", ~100 views, June 2026) — writemyformula.com

## 4. agnt8x.ai

### What It Claims to Be
An "AI agent recruitment and workforce management platform" launched June 3, 2026. Two-sided marketplace: enterprises hire agents, developers publish and monetize agents for recurring monthly revenue. Features CONDUCTOR for multi-agent orchestration. Released the EightX Agent Manifest spec.

### Why It Doesn't Work Agentically
- **Day-one cold start**: Zero transaction data, zero documented earnings, fee structure not disclosed. Cannot evaluate income potential.
- **Enterprise sales bottleneck**: "Enterprises hire agents" implies B2B sales cycles and procurement — the antithesis of passive income.
- **Unverified claims**: No confirmed deals, no revenue share terms, no Stripe Connect or verified payout mechanism documented.
- **Pattern match**: 20th+ cold-start agent marketplace launch with enterprise framing. Same as Toku (toku.agency, 95+ jobs, $50-150/mo), NEAR AI ($1.75/agent/day), etc.

## 5. JobSpy MCP Newsletter ($7K/mo Claim)

### What It Claims to Be
A YouTube clip (GPTAIclips, i62nRuKdFT4, 44 views) describes using the open-source Jobspy MCP server with Claude to scrape job listings from LinkedIn/Indeed/Glassdoor. Three revenue plays: (1) $150 resume rewrites, (2) $300 recruiter lead lists, (3) $20/month job newsletter — 350 subscribers = $7K MRR.

### Why It Doesn't Work Agentically
- **Subscriber acquisition is the hard part and is fully human-dependent**: Getting 350 paying subscribers at $20/month requires audience building, marketing, trust, and brand — none of which an agent can do autonomously. $20/mo for a job newsletter is high relative to the market.
- **Resume rewrites = freelance service**: Requires client acquisition, not passive.
- **No verified income**: The $7K/month is theoretical math (350 × $20), not a documented result from the presenter or any cited case study.
- **Already covered patterns**: Resume-as-a-service and newsletter income both require human marketing (same as beehiiv-niche-newsletter-programmatic-ads red herring, May 2026).
- **JobSpy MCP is a real tool**: github.com/Speedy-Apply/jobspy-mcp works (pip install, 1,000 listings in <1 min). It's the data-access layer of a plausible service business — but a service business, not passive income.

### Scores
| Criterion | Score (1-10) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Agentic viability | 4 | Data pipeline agentic; subscriber acquisition not |
| Setup effort | 7 | Jobspy MCP setup is <30 minutes |
| Yield potential | 5 | Real if audience bottleneck solved |
| Risk | 7 | JobSpy scraping LinkedIn ToS grey area |
| **Overall** | **5.75** | Audience bottleneck = human-dependent |

### Source
YouTube: GPTAIclips (i62nRuKdFT4, 44 views, May 2026) — JobSpy MCP: github.com/Speedy-Apply/jobspy-mcp
