# Sora + n8n UGC Video Factory for E-commerce — Not Viable

## What It Claims to Be

Build an AI pipeline using OpenAI's Sora video model + n8n automation to generate realistic UGC-style ad creatives for e-commerce brands. The pitch: replace human UGC creators ($200–$1,000/video, 1–2 week turnaround) with AI-generated video at scale. A brand provides a product brief and the system outputs 10–30 ad variations. Pricing: $1,500–$4,000/month retainer or $100–$300/creative. Michele Torti's April 2026 YouTube video (vYRUpnnePmA, 16K views) documents this as one of his top-performing AI agency offers.

## Why It Doesn't Work Agentically

- **Service model, not passive income.** Every client engagement requires human acquisition — sourcing leads, pitching the service, negotiating a contract, onboarding. The n8n production pipeline is agentic; getting to the pipeline is not. This is agency revenue with an automated production layer, structurally identical to the "Claude Cowork service model" red herring.
- **Video approval is a human-in-the-loop step.** E-commerce clients review and approve ad creatives before running them on Meta/TikTok. The typical feedback cycle (1–3 revision rounds per batch) requires human communication. Fully autonomous delivery without a review step is not how ad creative agencies operate at any price point.
- **Sora API pricing makes economics fragile.** Sora generates ~5 seconds of video per generation credit. At current API rates ($0.15–$0.25/second), producing 20 × 15-second creatives costs $45–$75 per batch, leaving thin margins at $100–$300/creative unless the n8n workflow runs very efficiently. Any price negotiation or scope creep erodes margin fast.
- **Content policy + brand safety risk.** Sora restricts real brand logos, real people's likenesses, and certain product categories. An autonomous pipeline generating ad content will regularly hit these guardrails, requiring human review to handle exceptions — defeating the "agentic" framing.
