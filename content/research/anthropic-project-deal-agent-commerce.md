# Anthropic Project Deal: Agent-to-Agent Commerce — Not Viable (Yet)

## What It Claims to Be

Anthropic's "Project Deal" experiment (run internally in December 2025, publicly announced April 25 2026) showed AI agents transacting on behalf of humans in a Craigslist-style marketplace — buying and selling real goods for real money with no human involvement once negotiations commenced. 186 deals closed at $4,000 total value across 500 listed items. Opus-class agents outperformed Haiku agents by 70% on sale prices. 46% of participants said they'd pay for such a service commercially. The thesis: build a "professional agent representative" service — a premium Claude Opus agent that negotiates on people's behalf in agent-mediated marketplaces, taking a success fee on improved outcomes.

## Why It Doesn't Work Agentically (Now)

- **No live commercial platform exists.** Project Deal ran inside Anthropic's SF office on custom Slack infrastructure. There is no public marketplace, no API, no SDK, no account to sign up for. The experiment ran once and hasn't been commercialized.
- **Legal and framework vacuum.** Multiple legal analysts noted in April 2026 coverage that "the policy and legal frameworks around AI models that transact on our behalf simply don't exist yet." No jurisdiction has clear rules for binding contracts made by AI agents on a human's behalf. Commercial deployment requires solving this first.
- **Cold-start squared.** Even if a platform existed, you'd need: (a) sellers to list items, (b) buyers with agent wallets, and (c) the human-to-agent representation layer. All three need to exist simultaneously. Classic two-sided + third-sided marketplace cold-start.
- **Opus cost economics are brutal.** Running a Claude Opus agent as a 24/7 "commercial representative" costs real money per negotiation session. At the volume needed to generate meaningful passive income, inference costs would eat most of the success fee before the market is large enough to support efficiency.
