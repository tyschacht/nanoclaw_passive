# Wave 38 Bundle — Not Viable

## Sources Evaluated

Six content pieces evaluated from YouTube, DEV.to, and Reddit (June 2026). All mapped to existing library entries or established red herring patterns.

---

### 1. Joshua Mayo — "4 Ways to Make Money With Claude AI That Nobody Is Talking About" (wz9CmUZ4jRg, 127K views, May 12 2026)

**What it claims:** Four "underground" Claude AI income methods: (1) micro digital worksheets, (2) AI-generated digital art sticker packs on Etsy, (3) faceless YouTube channels via Higgsfield/ElevenLabs, (4) Claude-generated spreadsheet templates on Etsy.

**Why it fails:** Methods 1 and 4 are Etsy digital products, already documented in [[hyper-niche-etsy-digital-products]] (VIABLE, 7.5/10). Method 3 is faceless YouTube — confirmed red herring since January 2026 enforcement. Method 2 (AI sticker packs) is the same Etsy pipeline but requires Canva background removal (30+ min of human polish per product, admitted by the creator: "there is going to be a little bit of human effort"). No new mechanism.

**Agentic viability: 5/10.** Etsy digital products have a real agentic path but it's already documented. Not a new report.

---

### 2. Ross Minchev — "How to Make Money With Claude AI in 2026 (One Method Nobody Talks About)" (PpnCkE4Zx2g, 26K views, May 23 2026)

**What it claims:** Five business models: (1) PromptBase prompt collections, (2) AI sticker packs and clip art on Etsy, (3) faceless YouTube with Runway/Synthesia, (4) no-code SaaS on Lovable + Webflow + Stripe, (5) AI video courses on Gumroad/Teachable.

**Why it fails:** Method 1 is AI prompt marketplaces — confirmed red herring (PromptBase: manual dashboard listing only, commoditized declining-value products). Method 2 is Etsy stickers — already covered. Method 3 is faceless YouTube — confirmed red herring. Method 4 is no-code SaaS — Lovable requires the visual editor (no-code builder red herring, same pattern as MindStudio). Method 5 is courses — irreducibly human-created and human-distributed.

**Agentic viability: 3-5/10 depending on method.** None new.

### 3. Sharbel A. — "5 Ways I Make Money With Hermes Agent" (2WZAcWtwoDI, channel: Sharbel A., May 18 2026)

**What it claims:** Five concrete workflows: (1) lead generation and outreach, (2) content research, (3) trend scouting, (4) prediction market research/alerts, (5) client operations management.

**Why it fails:** The video *explicitly* disclaims autonomous income: *"Hermes does not remove the need for a real offer. It does not remove the need for you to figure out distribution. It does not remove the need for you to talk to customers."* All five methods are productivity amplifiers for humans who already have income-generating work — they reduce operator time but require human distribution, client acquisition, and/or judgment for revenue to materialize. Method 4 (prediction market research/alerts) is closest to agentic but the monetization path described is "sell a private research feed" — which requires human subscribers. The existing [[agentic-niche-alert-subscription]] report (VIABLE, 6.75/10) covers the subscription side more rigorously.

**Important channel note:** Sharbel A. is a HIGH-VALUE source for specific technical agent builds (Polymarket bot tutorials, pixel dev loops). His "5 ways with framework X" format produces service-model content. Distinguish between his technical build videos (evaluate those) vs. his framework overview videos (skip, they describe human-in-the-loop services). This is the first time Sharbel's content maps to a red herring pattern — it's format-specific, not channel-wide.

**Agentic viability: 4/10.** Service model throughout.

### 4. DEV.to — "Can an AI Agent Really Generate Passive Income? My Honest 30-Day Test" (June 2026)

**What it found:** Using OpenClaw for 30 days to attempt passive income. Results: zero direct income. Value extracted: 30-40 hours saved ("time dividend"). Trading scaffolding built but autonomous execution declined. Content articles written but zero distribution/audience. Agent required periodic supervision (12+ silent failures/month).

**Bottom line:** *"An AI agent will not generate passive income for you in the way the hype implies."* Instead, agents amplify productivity for people already doing income-generating work.

**Value:** Confirms the library's filter is correct. "Time dividend" is the accurate framing for productivity-amplifier agent use cases. True agentic passive income requires a specific transaction-based mechanism (maker rebates, MCP per-call fees, API marketplace, digital products with agentic listing path) — not general-purpose agent deployment.

---

### 5. Greg Isenberg — "The Next $100B Market: Selling to AI Agents" (MlptIfpoLlw, 42K views, June 2 2026)

**What it claims:** Macro trend video about building "agent-native" infrastructure. Agents are becoming internet customers; opportunities exist in agent identity, agent inboxes (AgentMail, YC-backed), agent wallets (Stripe), agent memory, agent procurement tools.

**Why it's not actionable:** Pure macro trend framing. No specific monetizable mechanism identified. The agent-native infrastructure layer (AgentMail, Stripe wallet, Circle Agent Stack) requires B2B startup-grade development — not a solo agent operator building for passive income. Confirms the x402/MPP ecosystem direction already in the library, but adds no new mechanism. AgentMail (YC-backed) is infrastructure for building agent-facing products — relevant as context for designing MCP servers and utility APIs, not as an income opportunity itself.

**Signal value:** The "billions of agent customers" framing validates building MPP-monetized utility APIs that agents will programmatically pay for. Directional confirmation only.
